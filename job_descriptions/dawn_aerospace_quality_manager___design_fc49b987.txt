Join us in developing a remotely piloted reusable spaceplane that combines the demanding performance of a rocket with the reusability of an aircraft.

We're looking for a Quality Manager - Design to join our Spaceplane Department and establish the certification basis and design assurance processes that underpin the design of Mk-II Aurora spaceplanes.

You will lead engagement with the civil aviation authority to agree the certification basis and acceptable means of compliance, then translate those agreements into clear guidance and practical design procedures for our engineers. You'll build and monitor the design organisation that gives the regulators, our customers, and ourselves confidence that the Spaceplane design is shown to comply with its requirements.

Our Spaceplane programme is based in Christchurch, New Zealand, and this opportunity is for people interested in joining us here in person.

What You'll Do

Establish the Certification Basis


Lead development of the Spaceplane certification basis, identifying applicable regulations, standards, and special conditions for a novel, rocket-powered, remotely piloted vehicle
Engage directly with the civil aviation authority to propose and agree acceptable means of compliance, resolving disagreements in a manner that builds trust and enables progress
Own the compliance plan and checklist, ensuring every requirement has an agreed method of compliance, owner, and supporting evidence
Lead development of changes to the Spaceplane certification basis to enable operations overseas where there may be different operating, equipment or safety requirements


Guide Design Engineers on Compliance


Advise design engineers on acceptable methods of compliance, setting clear expectations for analysis, test, inspection, and documentation
Review compliance documents, test plans, and reports to ensure they are complete, succinct, and acceptable to the authority
Mentor engineers on certification and design assurance, building the team's capability to show compliance independently


Own the Design Quality System


Develop and maintain Spaceplane design quality procedures aligned with AS9100D and design organisation approval expectations
Provide input to the engineering teams on acceptable methods for configuration management, design change control, design review, and release processes, with traceability from requirements to verification evidence
Audit design activities and own design corrective actions, halting the release of design data where needed


Requirements

What We're Looking For


You are an experienced certification engineer, design assurance engineer, or aerospace quality manager, comfortable presenting compliance approaches to a regulator and equally comfortable working through with design engineers how a requirement can practically be met. We expect strong experience in several of the following areas:
Establishing certification bases and acceptable means of compliance with civil aviation authorities (e.g. CAA NZ, FAA, CASA, EASA, ESA)
Aircraft, launch vehicle or UAS certification and design compliance planning (e.g. 14 CFR Part 21/23/25/437/450, CS-23/25)
Design organisation approvals (e.g. Part 21J, ODA, Part 148) and AS9100D design and development controls
Configuration management, requirements traceability, system safety programme, system safety or software/hardware development assurance (e.g. ARP4754, DO-178C, DO-254, RCC-319)


What Makes You Successful


You turn vague or ill-fitting certification requirements into clear direction for engineers, even where there is little precedent and the application of requirements to spaceplane technology is unclear
You build trust with regulators and reach agreement on pragmatic, defensible means of compliance
You balance safety, quality, technical risk, cost, and schedule, and are creative in finding better paths to certification
You take ownership of problems and are comfortable in a development environment where the spaceplane and its processes continue to evolve


Benefits

Why Join Us?

This is an opportunity to help develop a first-of-its-kind aerospace vehicle operating at the intersection of aviation and spaceflight.

You'll have genuine ownership of critical vehicle functions, work alongside highly capable engineers, and help solve technical challenges that don't exist anywhere else in the industry. Your work will directly influence how we demonstrate safety, reliability, and operational readiness as we take the vehicle from development into routine operations.

Diversity & Inclusion

At Dawn Aerospace, we believe that diverse teams build better solutions. We are committed to creating an inclusive environment where people of all backgrounds, identities, and experiences are valued and supported to do their best work.

About Dawn Aerospace

Dawn Aerospace is a space transportation company with a purpose.

Our mission is to enable the next generation of space users by providing more sustainable and scalable ways to access and move around in space. We believe this access, in the form of a transportation network, will fundamentally change what's possible from space. We currently focus on three areas of space transportation:


In-space propulsion We're a leading supplier of turnkey green propulsion systems for various classes of satellites. Our systems utilise safe, accessible propellants and we have over 170 of these propulsion units on more than 40 operational satellites already. A further 200 units are in production
In-space servicing Coupled with our propulsion expertise, we are ensuring that our customers' satellites are refuellable. In the coming years, Dawn will offer a refueling service to enable life extension of customer satellites. This new market is aligned with our sustainability goal to ensue a clean and safe space for everyone!
Spaceplane We are developing the fastest and highest-flying aircraft ever to take off from a runway, combining the extreme performance of rocket propulsion with the reusability of conventional airplanes to enable high-frequency, low-cost access to sub-orbital space


Our international teams are distributed across four locations: the Netherlands, France, New Zealand, and the USA.
