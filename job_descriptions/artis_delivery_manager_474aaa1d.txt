Katso, kuka Artis on palkattu tähän rooliin

Hae 
Tallenna

You'll own how engineering work gets into the teams, how it's planned against real capacity, and how progress is reported, and you'll be accountable for all three. When the business asks "when will it land, and what does it push back?", you're the person with the answer and the evidence behind it.

Why the role exists. Requests reach engineering from Product, Commercial, Customer Success and leadership by different routes. It's hard to say what a new request will displace, so dates are hard to give and progress is hard to show. Our engineers, team leads and technical direction are strong. What's missing is one person who owns delivery end to end.

What it isn't. This isn't an engineering manager role. Team leads manage their people, and technical decisions stay with them and the CTO. Your focus is outcomes, not activity: shipping what we committed to, when we committed to it, and holding up the mirror to Product and Engineering alike, with evidence.

What you own, and answer for

Four areas are yours outright. In each, you decide how it works and you're accountable for the result.

1. Intake: one front door

Design and run the single route by which all work reaches engineering, whatever its source.

Before anything is considered for planning, it has a business owner, a rough size and a clear reason for its timing.

For every new request, set out what it would displace, so the trade-off is made in the open.

You answer for: nothing reaching the teams outside intake, and every live request being visible in one place.



2. Capacity and plans

Keep a live view of what each team can really take on, including the load from support, security work and tech debt.

Combine Product's priority order with the team leads' estimates into plans with forecast dates and a stated level of confidence.

Re-plan openly the moment scope, priority or capacity shifts.

You answer for: forecasts that hold. Your plans are judged on how often committed dates are met, and on how early you flag it when they won’t be..



3. Visibility and reporting

Be engineering's single voice on delivery status: what's in flight, what's next and what's at risk.

Run a regular delivery review with Product, Commercial and Customer Success, and a short report leadership actually reads.

Raise risk as soon as you see it, with a recommendation and the cost of each option.

You answer for: no surprises. The business hears about slippage from you, early.



4. The delivery system and releases

Own the end-to-end workflow, from accepted request to released change, including limits on work in progress.

Measure the flow (lead time, throughput, unplanned work entering mid-cycle) and fix the bottlenecks it reveals. It measures the system, never individuals.

Coordinate releases across teams, so Customer Success and Commercial know what's shipping before customers see it.

Sit in on scoping between senior engineers and Product, so what's agreed lands in the plan.

You answer for: a process that gets lighter, faster and more predictable each quarter.



Intake: Run the single route by which all work reaches engineering, making sure each request has a business owner, a rough size, a reason for its timing, and a clear view of what it would displace.

Capacity and plans: Keep a live view of what each team can really take on, and turn Product's priorities and team leads' estimates into plans with forecast dates and confidence levels.

Visibility and reporting: Act as engineering's single voice on delivery status, run a regular delivery review with Product, Commercial and Customer Success, and flag risks early with a recommendation.

Delivery system and releases: Own the workflow from accepted request to released change, measure flow to fix bottlenecks, and coordinate releases across teams.

You'll be

Outcome-driven. You care about what shipped, not how busy people were.

Direct and collaborative. You'll challenge Product and Engineering with equal comfort, using evidence, without making it personal.

Calm under competing demands, and good at saying "yes, and here's what moves".

Someone who earns influence without positional authority.



Nice to have

Experience where customers depend on frequent, reliable releases, such as trading, fintech or other time-critical B2B platforms.

Experience measuring how AI-assisted tooling changes delivery flow, beyond whether people like it.

Software engineering background. Enough technical literacy to follow a scoping conversation though technical decisions stay with the team leads and CTO.
